01 / Start with value

All tasks finished.
Problem still unsolved.

A team delivers every planned task. Users still cannot solve their problem. Was the work successful?

Facilitator
Ask everyone to type A–D in chat, then press Enter together. Click an answer to reveal the explanation. Ask: “What would success look like for the user?”
02 / Work with uncertainty

Make a small bet.
Learn from reality.

Scrum is a lightweight framework for complex work. Short feedback cycles help us decide what to do next.

01 / TRANSPARENCY

Make it visible.

Use shared goals, visible work and clear quality expectations.

02 / INSPECTION

Check the evidence.

Look at what users can do with the result. Ask what changed.

03 / ADAPTATION

Change the plan.

Use what you learned to choose a better next step.

Reveal the five Scrum values

Commitment · Focus · Openness · Respect · Courage

Try it: share an uncertainty openly, listen respectfully and commit to a small experiment.

Where does uncertainty show up in our daily work?

Facilitator · 04–09
Take two short examples from different functions. Use a customer, operations or internal-service example to keep the session relevant beyond software.
03 / Three accountabilities

Different contributions.
One Scrum Team.

PO

Product Owner

Maximizes product value and is accountable for effective Product Backlog management.

SM

Scrum Master

Establishes Scrum and supports the Scrum Team’s effectiveness.

DEV

Developers

Create a usable Increment, plan their work and uphold quality.

Does “Developer” mean programmer?

No. In Scrum, Developers contribute the skills needed to create the product, regardless of technical or functional specialty.

Who decides who does what, when and how?

The Scrum Team is self-managing. A Scrum Master supports effectiveness rather than assigning everyone’s tasks.

Facilitator · 09–15
Ask for examples of useful skills: research, writing, analysis, design, testing, operations. Temporary exercise accountabilities are independent of seniority.
04 / A quick scenario

Who creates
the delivery plan?

The team has discussed valuable work. Who creates the Sprint Backlog, including the plan for delivering it?

Facilitator
Collect a chat vote before revealing. Clarify: the whole Scrum Team collaborates in Sprint Planning; Developers select work with the Product Owner and create the delivery plan.
05 / Events

A Sprint is a
learning cycle.

Sprint: a fixed period of one month or less, containing the other four events.

PlanningAgree why the Sprint is valuable, what can be Done and how to deliver it.
Daily ScrumDevelopers inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt their plan. 15 minutes.
ReviewThe Scrum Team and stakeholders inspect the outcome and discuss future adaptations.
RetrospectiveThe Scrum Team improves quality and effectiveness.
Review or Retrospective?

Review: What did we learn about the product, users and what comes next?
Retrospective: How can we improve how we work?

Facilitator · 15–21
The Sprint itself is an event, giving five events in total. A Sprint Review is a working session, not only a demo or a release gate.
06 / Shared information

Make the work
understandable.

Predict the commitment, then open each card.

Product Backlog → ?

Commitment: Product Goal.
An ordered, evolving list of product improvements, guided by a longer-term objective.

Sprint Backlog → ?

Commitment: Sprint Goal.
The goal, selected Product Backlog items and actionable delivery plan.

Increment → ?

Commitment: Definition of Done.
A usable result that meets the required quality measures.

Almost finished is useful information — but work that fails the Definition of Done is not part of the Increment.

Facilitator
Keep goals distinct from task lists. A board is useful for visibility, but columns alone do not constitute a Sprint Backlog.
07 / Exercise briefing

Help a new colleague
start confidently.

Build a one-page First-Day Survival Guide. A newcomer should find their first meeting, get help and complete one useful action.

01 / GOAL

Choose an outcome.

What should a newcomer be able to do after using your guide?

02 / BUILD

Make it usable.

Use simple text and shapes. Use fictional people and example meeting details.

03 / LEARN

Test and improve.

Ask someone to use the guide without coaching. Adapt using their feedback.

Exercise Definition of Done

Facilitator · 21–25
Assign one PO, one SM and remaining participants as Developers in each room. In small groups, make sure at least two people build. Each group is an independent simulation, not three teams sharing one product. The learning rounds compress Scrum and do not use real event timeboxes.

Facilitator · 25–45
Send participants to rooms for 20 minutes. Use a broadcast message at minute 9 to reveal the feedback. A Developer temporarily acts as a newcomer during each review: this is a user test proxy, not a full stakeholder Review. Ask each room to return with a 60-second showcase.
09 / 20-minute simulation

Build. Test.
Improve. Repeat.

Reveal user feedback after the first review

“I am joining remotely from another time zone. I cannot tell when the meeting starts or what to do if my access fails.”

Discuss what to change, what to keep and what can wait.

Facilitator
The phases total exactly 20 minutes. Announce the current phase in breakout rooms. The mid-build progress check illustrates inspection; a real Daily Scrum happens each working day, not once per short learning round. Keep the surprise hidden until after the first user test.

Use one minute per team, then three minutes to compare insights. Ask which collaboration improvement actually helped. Avoid ranking teams by output volume.
11 / Individual challenge

Ready for
the knowledge check?

Facilitator · 51–57
Five questions; allow around 45 seconds per question. A shared-screen score reflects the facilitator’s selections, not individual results. Participants can open their own copy to answer independently. Discuss the most frequently missed topic.
12 / From workshop to work

One small experiment.
A useful next step.

Choose one: clarify our goal, make work visible, get feedback earlier or agree what “Done” means.

Success: a usable guide, a clear lesson from feedback and one improvement to try.

Facilitator · 57–60
Ask each team to agree one realistic experiment. Aim for 4/5 in the quiz as a learning signal, not a certification. Thank everyone and share the Scrum Guide.
